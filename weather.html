Packing

The swing is the thing. A Bryce sunrise at 15 F and an Angels Landing climb at 50 F happen two days apart, and the climb makes its own heat. Pack layers you can shed, not one warm coat.

Ski jackets: yes, with a caveat

  • An insulated ski jacket is right for the standing-still parts: Bryce rim at sunrise, viewpoints, the horseback ride, the car. Bring it.
  • It is too warm for the climbs. Navajo Loop gains 600 ft, Angels Landing 1,500 ft, Cassidy Arch 700 ft. In an insulated jacket you sweat on the way up and freeze at the top. For hikes wear a base layer, a fleece or light puffy, and a shell, and stuff the puffy in the pack for the summit.
  • If your ski jacket is an uninsulated shell, it is the perfect hiking shell. If it is insulated, bring a separate light rain shell too.

Upper body

  • 2 merino or synthetic base layers (no cotton).
  • 1 fleece or grid-fleece mid layer.
  • 1 light puffy (down or synthetic) that packs small.
  • 1 waterproof-breathable shell, hood that fits over a hat.
  • Ski jacket for mornings and viewpoints.

Legs and feet

  • Hiking pants, 2 pairs. Base-layer bottoms for the Bryce morning and the horseback ride.
  • Rain pants if the forecast turns wet, otherwise optional.
  • Waterproof hiking boots, broken in. Wool socks, 4 pairs plus a liner pair.
  • Microspikes (Kahtoola or similar), one pair each. Required for Bryce and the shaded Zion trails, useful on Cassidy Arch. Not crampons, not Yaktrax.
  • Gaiters optional; the snow is shallow this year.

Head and hands

  • Warm beanie, plus a sun hat. The sun at 8,000 ft in December still burns.
  • Warm gloves plus thin liner gloves. Angels Landing chains in the cold need grip, so liners with a textured palm.
  • Sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm.
  • Hand warmers, a few pairs, for the sunrise and the ride.

Activity-specific

  • Narrows. The outfitter supplies dry suit, neoprene socks, canyon boots and a stick. Under the suit wear fleece or synthetic top and bottom, warm socks, and a wool hat. Nothing cotton. A dry bag for the phone.
  • Horseback. Long pants, boots with a bit of heel (hiking boots are fine), ski jacket, gloves. Nothing flapping.
  • Canyoneering. Outfitter supplies helmet, harness and rope. Wear layers you do not mind scuffing and gloves you do not love. Shell on top; the slot canyons are cold and sometimes drippy.
  • Angels Landing. Permit on the phone, microspikes, liner gloves, 1.5 L water each, a bar. Start at first light.

Daypack, always

  • Headlamp each, with fresh batteries. Sunset is at 5:05 and the Dec 3 start is before sunrise.
  • 2 L water each, lunch and bars. Few places to buy food between the gate and the trail.
  • Trekking poles, collapsible. Help on icy descents.
  • Battery pack and a short cable. Cold drains phones fast; keep the phone in an inside pocket.
  • Paper map of each park (free at the gate) and the NPS app with Zion, Bryce and Capitol Reef downloaded for offline use.
  • Small first-aid kit, blister tape.

Car and admin

  • Ice scraper and a small snow brush. Rental cars often have neither.
  • Windshield washer fluid gets used up on slushy roads; top it up at the first gas stop.
  • America the Beautiful pass (bought at the Bryce gate Dec 2), driver's licences, the credit card the rental is on.
  • A real blanket in the car, plus water and snacks, for a road closure wait.
  • One town outfit for Salt Lake City on Dec 8.

Leave at home

  • Tire chains. Hertz forbids them. Snowshoes and skis, not needed with 3 in of snow.
  • Cotton anything for the trails.
